The situation

A new law says: if you run an AI system, keep records.

Write down what the AI was asked, what it answered, which model ran, and when. That is the EU AI Act, and it is in force. The trouble is that most teams cannot tell you whether their records actually pass.

Finding out the old way means paying a law firm to read your code and logs for a few weeks. Call it five or six figures, and a report that is stale the moment your system changes. This tool does that gap analysis in seconds, from the logs you already have.

Why you can trust it

It shipped wrong once. That is the whole reason it is careful now.

Version 0.14 went out with roughly 60% of its EU AI Act requirements fabricated. A language model had projected software-engineering habits onto legal text; the output looked authoritative and was simply made up. A maintainer of a real open-source AI project caught it.

So it was torn down and rebuilt around one rule: no requirement ships unless it quotes the exact clause, "Article 12(2)(a)", and has been checked page by page against the primary legal text. The more abstract the jump from legal words to a code check, the higher the odds a model invented the connection. So the discipline is now backwards from how it started: write the legal quote first, then ask whether the check actually follows from those exact words. If it does not, it is labelled guidance, not law.

How it works

There is no AI in the checking. A field is present, or it is not.

For each requirement, the tool asks one question of each event in your logs: is this field there. It counts how many events have it, and divides. Take Article 12(1), which says to keep automatic logs. The tool checks whether each event carries a timestamp. Run it on a real 410-event session and it finds 331 of them. That is the number. Same logs in, same answer out, every time. Nothing to hallucinate.

The score

One number would be a lie, so you get three.

Some rules are the law. Some are best practice. A single "85% compliant" lets a missing legal requirement hide behind a pile of easy bonus points. So the checks are split, and a failure in the first tier cannot be bought back by the third.

Tier 1: Legal Compliance

Binary pass or fail on the checks the law prescribes exactly. Article 12(3) biometric fields, Article 19 retention, Article 50 disclosure. No partial credit.

Tier 2: Structural Evidence

Coverage for capability-level requirements, where the law asks that you be able to do something. General logging, NIST monitoring, system documentation.

Tier 3: Quality

Best-practice observability that is smart but not legally required. Token counting, trace linkage, evaluation capture.
